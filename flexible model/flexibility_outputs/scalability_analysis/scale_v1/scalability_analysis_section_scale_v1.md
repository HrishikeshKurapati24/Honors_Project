### Flexibility Analysis

Figure X summarizes the flexibility behavior of the proposed omics encoder across five complementary stress tests. In the subset-cardinality experiment, increasing the number of input modalities did not yield monotonic performance gains: mean AUC varied only from 0.9189 to 0.9206, and mean AUPR varied from 0.8883 to 0.8912, across one to seven modalities. Notably, the best single-modality configuration, transcriptomics_expression, achieved 0.9211 AUC and 0.8920 AUPR, exceeding the full seven-omics configuration (0.9190 AUC, 0.8884 AUPR). This indicates that the current omics collection is not limited by encoder capacity alone, and that modality identity contributes more strongly than raw modality count.

The pathway-sharding experiment isolates structural flexibility by increasing the number of pathway-derived inputs while holding the underlying biological source fixed. When the single pathway source was split into 1, 2, 4, 8, and 16 pseudo-modalities, AUC changed from 0.9189 to 0.9189 and AUPR changed from 0.8880 to 0.8883. Mean fold runtime remained tightly bounded between 296.5s and 303.0s, and parameter count remained constant at 7.43M. These results show that the encoder can absorb a larger number of modality channels without measurable instability or collapse in predictive quality.

The missing-modality study demonstrates inference-time robustness under partial observation. Relative to the full seven-omics reference (0.9193 AUC, 0.8888 AUPR), randomly removing one modality reduced mean performance to 0.9094 AUC and 0.8699 AUPR, randomly removing two modalities reduced it to 0.9011 and 0.8534, and randomly removing four modalities still retained 0.9023 AUC and 0.8568 AUPR on average. Category-specific removal showed that dropping both epigenomic modalities remained comparatively mild (0.9144 AUC, 0.8802 AUPR), whereas removing transcriptomics produced the steepest degradation (0.8948 AUC, 0.8383 AUPR). This pattern suggests that the encoder is robust to incomplete modality availability, while also revealing that transcriptomics remains the single most informative source within the current data regime.

The noisy-modality experiment further tests whether the encoder degrades when irrelevant inputs are appended rather than removed. Adding up to eight permuted noise modalities changed AUC by only -0.0005 to +0.0005 relative to the zero-noise baseline, and changed AUPR by only -0.0003 to +0.0012. At the same time, memory consumption increased from 2.39 GiB to 2.47 GiB, and parameter count increased from 47.73M to 49.94M. Therefore, the encoder shows clear tolerance to irrelevant modality expansion, with only a modest compute overhead and no corresponding loss in predictive discrimination.

Finally, the integrated compute-performance frontier consolidates experiments with complete telemetry, namely subset cardinality, pathway sharding, and noisy-modality injection. Across these settings, mean fold runtime remained within 296.5s to 325.5s and peak GPU memory remained within 1.52 to 2.47 GiB, while AUC stayed concentrated near 0.919. The pathway-sharding regime was the lightest configuration overall (7.43M parameters, 1.52 GiB, 298.6s mean fold time), whereas noisy-modality injection was the heaviest (49.00M parameters, 2.43 GiB, 304.8s). Taken together, these results support the claim that the proposed encoder is operationally flexible: it tolerates increasing, missing, and noisy modality sets with stable predictive behavior and predictable compute growth, even though the current legacy omics dataset itself does not reward naive addition of more modalities.
